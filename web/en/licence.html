Where the data come from

{{t:web.brand}} draws nothing by hand and invents nothing. Every map is built from open data published by public bodies and by the OpenStreetMap community:

  • Terrain and vegetation – airborne laser scanning (LiDAR) and elevation models from the national mapping agency of the country. They give the shape of the ground, cliffs and boulders, and the height of the growth.
  • Paths, roads, buildings, water, fences – OpenStreetMap, completed where available by national topographic and technical maps (in Czechia ZABAGED® and the regional digital technical map).
  • Templates – orthophoto, cadastre or hillshade from the same providers, and optionally Sentinel-2 satellite images. They are only placed under the map for drawing.

Each country uses the data of its own providers; the full list and the licences are under data by country.

How the map is made

The data are downloaded only for the area you select. The server computes contours, cliffs, knolls and vegetation classes from the elevation models, combines them with the vector layers and turns everything into the symbols of ISOM, ISSprOM or ISMTBOM. The result is a ZIP with an editable .omap / .ocd map and the source layers, georeferenced in the national coordinate system of the country.

The ZIP stays available for a limited time and is then deleted. The output is a working base map for drawing, not a precise survey: vegetation shows height and density of growth, not runnability, and everything is meant to be checked and finished in the terrain.

Generated map (job output)

The PNG, .omap, DXF and shapefiles from a job are licensed under CC BY 4.0: for further use (printing, events, publications, sharing) you must credit the source. This also applies to a map redrawn and completed in the field from the base map: the credit must make clear that the map used {{t:web.brand}}.

Minimum credit on the map / in the legend: the line printed in ABOUT_OMAPIC.txt of your ZIP, together with the web address. It depends on the country of the area (see data by country); for Czechia:

Base map: {{t:web.brand}} ({{SITE_HOST}}) · ČÚZK · OSM, [year]

[year] is the year the data used are current to (elevation / technical map / OSM), not necessarily the year you printed the map. If you do not know the exact date of a layer, the year you ran the job in {{t:web.brand}} is enough (typically this year). The output is a working base map for drawing, not a precise survey. More detailed credits for the sources below are welcome (e.g. “ZABAGED® © ČÚZK”).

ČÚZK data

The digital terrain model DMR 5G, the surface model DMP OK and the topographic database ZABAGED® are provided by the Land Survey Office / ČÚZK (Czech Office for Surveying, Mapping and Cadastre) as open data under CC BY 4.0.

  • ČÚZK Geoportal
  • openzu – DMR 5G
  • openzu – DMP OK
  • ČÚZK terms for providing spatial data (in Czech)

On printed output: ČÚZK, [year] (year by how current the data are).

Regional DTM (DMVS)

Surveyed features (sidewalks, roads, buildings, stairs, fences, bridges…) come from the regional digital technical map, maintained by the regions and published by ČÚZK in the DMVS information system (public ZPS datasets). The public data are available free of charge and without registration (“without any conditions”); the DMVS information system was built with IROP funding (co-financed by the European Union).

No explicit licence (such as CC BY) is stated for these datasets, and the ČÚZK terms (CC BY 4.0, “ČÚZK, [year]”) do not explicitly cover the regional DTM. Use of the data in {{t:web.brand}} outputs has, however, been confirmed. We credit the source in the spirit of those terms: Regional DTM · ČÚZK / DMVS, [year].

  • DMVS – data provision (in Czech)
  • DMVS information system project (IROP, in Czech)

Sentinel-2 satellite images

The optional template references/sentinel2_*.jpg comes from the Copernicus programme (ESA / EU); Sentinel-2 L2A data are free to use with attribution and are downloaded from AWS Open Data (Element84 earth-search). They are not used to draw the map. Credit: Contains modified Copernicus Sentinel data [year].

  • Sentinel-2 L2A COGs on AWS Open Data

Inspiration

{{t:web.brand}} was loosely inspired by the Karttapullautin project and then went its own way: vegetation from LiDAR return density, cliffs and rocks from the DMR, GDAL contours.

Symbol sets

The ISOM 2017-2 (Revision 6) and ISSprOM 2019-2 (Revision 6) sets are based on the official OCAD symbol sets; the basic symbol names follow the official Czech translations of the specifications (ČSOS), sub-symbol names are derived. Likewise, the ISMTBOM 2022 set for MTBO is based on the official OCAD set; names of symbols without an official Czech translation are derived.
